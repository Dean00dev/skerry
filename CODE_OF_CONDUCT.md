# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is acting in good faith. Argue about the
code, not the person writing it.

## What is expected

- Take criticism of your work without taking it personally, and give criticism
  of someone else's work without making it personal.
- Accept that "no" is a complete answer to a feature request, and that a
  maintainer saying no is not an insult.
- Be patient with people who are new, working in a second language, using
  assistive technology, or contributing from a phone. All four are normal here.
- Say what you mean plainly. Sarcasm travels badly across a text-only medium.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, intimidation, or sustained disruption
- Personal attacks, or comments demeaning someone for who they are rather than
  what they wrote
- Sexualised language or imagery, in any context
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission
- Deliberately submitting malicious code, malicious fixtures, or a report
  crafted to waste a maintainer's time

## Scope

This applies in issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, and any
other space where someone is representing this project.

## Enforcement

Report a problem through this repository's private vulnerability reporting
form or by opening an issue if the matter is not sensitive. Reports are handled
by the maintainer, Dean Egan.

Responses, depending on severity: a private word, a public correction, edited
or removed content, or a block from the repository. The maintainer's decision
is final and does not come with a right of appeal, because this is a small
open-source project and not a court.

This document is written for this project rather than adopted wholesale. If you
prefer a formal standard, the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org)
is the widely used one and nothing here contradicts it.
